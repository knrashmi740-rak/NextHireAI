Compound Interest

Understand interest calculation where interest is added to the principal periodically.

📘 Concept Summary

Compound Interest is the interest calculated on the initial principal amount as well as the accumulated interest from previous periods. Unlike simple interest, the interest keeps increasing because every year's interest is added back to the amount.

⭐ Important Terms

  • Principal (P): Initial amount invested or borrowed.
  • Rate (R): Percentage of interest charged per year.
  • Time (T): Duration for which money is invested.
  • Amount: Total money after adding compound interest.

⭐ Important Formulas

Amount = P × (1 + R/100)n
Compound Interest = Amount - Principal
For 2 years:

CI - SI = P × (R/100)2

💡 Shortcuts & Tricks

  • Compound Interest is always greater than Simple Interest for the same time period.
  • For half-yearly compounding, rate becomes R/2 and time becomes 2T.
  • For quarterly compounding, rate becomes R/4 and time becomes 4T.
  • Convert percentage into fraction for faster calculation.
  • Remember: Interest is calculated on the updated amount every year.

📝 Example

Find the compound interest on ₹1000 at 10% per annum for 2 years.

Amount = 1000 × (1 + 10/100)2

Amount = 1000 × (1.1)2

Amount = 1000 × 1.21

Amount = ₹1210

Compound Interest = 1210 - 1000

CI = ₹210

🎯 Placement Tips

  • Questions are commonly asked in TCS, Infosys, Wipro and Accenture aptitude tests.
  • Learn the difference between Simple Interest and Compound Interest.
  • Practice yearly and half-yearly compounding problems.
